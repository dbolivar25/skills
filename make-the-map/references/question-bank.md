# Decision-Map Question Bank

Load this reference only when the map stalls or the user explicitly asks to be
grilled. Select the smallest set of questions that exposes a missing part of the
current map. Ask one question at a time and include the recommended answer.

Do not walk through this file mechanically.

## Name the decision

- What decision are we actually making?
- Who owns it, and who only needs to be heard?
- What is inside scope, outside scope, and deliberately undecided?
- What happens if the decision is delayed?
- Are two apparently competing options answering different questions?

## Build the coordinate system

- Which dimensions make the options meaningfully different?
- What constraints eliminate otherwise plausible options?
- Which stakeholders are imagining different maps?
- What relationship is still difficult to express in prose?
- What table, sequence, diagram, or named model would make it precise?

## Find value and risk

- What outcome is the decision meant to protect or produce?
- Which risk are we reducing?
- Which risk are we accepting in exchange?
- Are we optimizing for user value, revenue, speed, execution certainty,
  operational safety, maintainability, adoption, or reversibility?
- What would failure look like after six months?

## Set the complexity boundary

- Which complexity belongs to the real problem?
- Which complexity did our implementation, tools, process, or organization add?
- What becomes false, hidden, brittle, or unsafe if we simplify further?
- How will users, operators, or developers reason about what remains?
- What could be deleted without changing the outcome?

## Test freshness and suitability

- What job does this option do well?
- Under what conditions does it break down?
- What ugly or inexpensive option might be suitable under the actual constraints?
- Which belief comes from current evidence, and which is an inherited heuristic?
- What primary source would most quickly confirm or refute the uncertain claim?
- What observation would change the recommendation?

## Account for adoption

- Who must approve, understand, operate, defend, or change behavior?
- Who gains burden or loses control under this option?
- Which resistance reveals a real risk rather than ordinary discomfort?
- Is this improvement worth spending political capital on?
- Which imperfection can be tolerated so the important change can land?

## Recommend or stop

- What credible alternative did we seriously consider?
- What hidden assumption could collapse the recommendation?
- What would a skeptical executive, reviewer, customer, or operator challenge?
- Can the decision be deferred, staged, or made reversible?
- What signal would show that the decision worked?
- Is an answer-changing question still open?
