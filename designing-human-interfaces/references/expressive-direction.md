# Expressive Direction

Use this reference for a genuinely open-ended surface that needs an expressive
direction: a marketing page, editorial experience, portfolio, launch surface,
demo, or blank-canvas product. Do not use it merely because the work is
frontend.

## Default gravity

The first plausible composition often reflects the model's defaults rather than
the product: safe type, interchangeable cards, evenly distributed emphasis, and
decoration standing in for an idea. Reject that first composition when it could
belong to any product with different copy.

The answer is not novelty. Find one product-specific visual idea derived from
the moment and facets. It may live in composition, typography, material,
interaction, imagery, information density, or motion. A conventional treatment
is valid when it expresses the product more truthfully than an unusual one.

## Expressive license

Before exploring, establish how much freedom the surface actually has:

- Which brand and product grammar already govern it?
- Which platform and category expectations form the floor?
- What must remain familiar for trust, comprehension, and accessibility?
- Where can expression carry meaning without weakening the task?

An established product surface usually grants less license than a campaign or
editorial surface. Treat departure from the existing grammar as a decision that
must earn its cost.

## Create range

Explore structurally different directions, not different style adjectives.
Vary the underlying idea across two or more of these axes:

- composition and reading order;
- typographic voice and information density;
- material, imagery, and depth;
- interaction model and continuity through the flow;
- motion behavior and its semantic job.

For each direction, state the product-specific idea, what makes it fit the
moment, what familiar expectation it preserves, and its likely failure mode.
Do not use font bans, fashionable effects, surprise, or visual loudness as a
substitute for a direction.

## Commit and deepen

Choose the direction whose idea best fits the moment and survives the floor,
grammar, accessibility, performance, and implementation constraints. Deepen
that idea consistently; do not accumulate unrelated flourishes after choosing.

Animation needs a behavioral or semantic job. Additional implementation
complexity must create a visible consequence important enough to own. If the
same idea survives with less machinery, prefer the simpler expression.

Completion criterion: the chosen direction has one product-specific visual
idea, differs structurally from the rejected default, preserves the necessary
floor, and contains no effect or complexity justified only by novelty.

## Failure modes

- **Stock rebellion** — replacing a generic template with a fashionable
  anti-template.
- **Aesthetic costume** — applying a named style without deriving it from the
  product moment.
- **Novelty tax** — making comprehension, accessibility, performance, or
  maintenance pay for surprise.
- **Flourish pile** — accumulating individually attractive effects that do not
  express the same idea.
