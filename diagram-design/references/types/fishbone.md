# Fishbone cause analysis

Use this to organize possible causes of one named effect. It supports investigation; its shape does not establish causation or prove there is exactly one root cause.

Define the effect concretely and identify the evidence window. Choose causal categories that fit the domain. Traditional 6M categories are a prompt, not required labels or permission to fill empty branches with generic speculation.

Place categories on major bones and specific causes on subordinate bones. Distinguish hypotheses, supported causes, ruled-out causes, and confirmed contributors using explicit labels or a declared legend. Several contributors can be supported at once. If a status is unknown, preserve that uncertainty rather than selecting a decorative focal root cause.

A horizontal spine with diagonal bones gives clear grouping. Derive label space from actual content and widen the canvas or split the view if branches become unreadable. Bone count, angles, and example coordinates are layout choices. Do not attach a cause to the wrong category to make it fit.

Verify each cause's category, wording, status, and evidence against the investigation. Inspect subordinate connectors and the effect label at reading size. Preserve important counterevidence and avoid presenting a list of plausible reasons as a settled diagnosis.
