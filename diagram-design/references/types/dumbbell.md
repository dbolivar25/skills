# Dumbbell charts

Use this to compare two measurements for each category on one quantitative axis. A connecting segment shows the gap between endpoints; it does not establish a trajectory or rate of change. If more than two ordered observations matter, use a line or small multiples.

Both endpoints use the same units, scale, and transformation. Choose bounds for the comparison the reader needs: an absolute magnitude comparison may need zero; a focused difference view may use a disclosed narrower range. Include every endpoint in the domain. For a constant or all-zero dataset, choose a finite domain appropriate to the units and make the constant values clear.

Distinguish endpoint identities with labels and redundant marks, such as filled and open dots. Keep every dot at its true coordinate. When endpoints coincide or nearly coincide, use a composite symbol, smaller dots, or exact value labels, without widening the measured gap. Place labels beside the left and right ends of the pair based on their actual positions; moving a label is different from moving a dot.

Show a known endpoint with a missing counterpart as incomplete. A missing pair is not a zero gap. State whether categories are sorted by signed change, absolute change, baseline, or domain order.

Independently calculate endpoint coordinates and signed differences from the source. Compare rendered positions, labels, and legend identity against those calculations, then inspect dense and coincident rows at delivery size. Optional data attributes can aid inspection but are not an independent oracle.

A dumbbell can use the bar chart’s axis and labeling craft; the current gallery has no dedicated dumbbell asset.
