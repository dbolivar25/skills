# Journey maps

Use this for a person's experience across stages: goals, actions, touchpoints, obstacles, and outcomes. Use process or sequence diagrams when the subject is system behavior rather than human experience.

Define the persona or participant, scenario, time frame, and evidence source. Choose stages from the observed or proposed journey. Separate current experience from a designed future experience. A touchpoint's presence does not establish satisfaction.

Use rows that answer the task: actions, channels, questions, pain, opportunities, or feelings. Feelings need evidence. Mark unknown sentiment instead of inventing a rating for every stage. If sentiment is qualitative, declare the ordinal labels; if it is measured, preserve the instrument and units. Do not join sparse observations with a smooth curve that implies unobserved emotional continuity.

Keep stage order and supporting quotes or evidence attached to their actual stage. A proposed opportunity is a recommendation, distinct from an observed obstacle. Multiple people or branches may need separate tracks so differences do not disappear into an average persona.

Verify stage coverage, source attribution, current-versus-proposed status, and any sentiment encoding. Inspect dense columns, evidence labels, unknown states, and the actual slide or document size.
