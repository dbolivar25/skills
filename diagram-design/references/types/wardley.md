# Wardley maps

Use this for strategic discussion of a value chain and component evolution. It is a contextual assessment, not a deployment topology or a measured maturity score.

Start with a user's need and the components supporting it. Vertical position represents visibility or proximity to that need; horizontal position represents the selected evolution stages, commonly genesis, custom-built, product/rental, and commodity/utility. State the assessment basis and context. The same technology can occupy different positions in different markets or uses.

Preserve real dependencies and uncertainty in placement. A tidy chain must not invent components or remove shared dependencies. Movement arrows are forecasts or proposed action, with their basis and status stated. Do not assume every component must move right or that position alone dictates a recommendation.

Keep component labels and dependencies readable. Use a legend for current, proposed, uncertain, or emphasized items when those distinctions appear. A chosen focal component is an argument to assess, not a mandatory feature of the form.

Verify the user anchor, dependency chain, evolution judgments, and recommendations against the source. Inspect ambiguous positions, crossing dependencies, and any current-versus-proposed distinction at the delivered size.
