# Annotations

Use an annotation for evidence, caveats, or interpretation that belongs beside a particular part of the diagram. It is not a substitute for a missing primary label or a place to hide uncertainty that changes the whole figure.

Keep the note near its subject without covering marks, nodes, or routes. A leader can clarify attachment; use a thin, distinct treatment without an arrowhead that could be mistaken for system flow. Separate observation, interpretation, and recommendation in the wording.

Typography follows the selected skin. An editorial italic can provide a secondary voice, but Instrument Serif is a gallery choice, not a required font or an Augment rule. Matter SQ italic is available in the Augment package. A neutral sans annotation is also valid.

Use as many notes as the real question needs while avoiding an unreadable perimeter. If notes dominate the figure, move detailed explanation into adjacent prose and retain visible caveats essential to interpretation. Check attachment, source certainty, contrast, and clipping at final size.
