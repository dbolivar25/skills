# Prose pattern clusters

Use these as diagnostic classes. A watched word is evidence only when its use,
frequency, and neighbors create a pattern.

## Inflated importance and promotion

Watch for claims that ordinary facts mark a pivotal shift, testify to a legacy,
reflect a broader movement, or reveal a vibrant landscape. Promotional
adjectives and notability lists often perform the same inflation.

Repair by stating the supported fact and its real consequence. Preserve genuine
significance when the source establishes it.

> Before: The launch marks a pivotal moment in the evolving analytics landscape.
>
> After: The launch gives analysts access to the new dataset.

## Vague authority and fabricated depth

Watch for unnamed experts, observers, industry reports, unsupported
attributions, and participial tails such as "ensuring reliability" or
"underscoring its importance." Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers and speculative gap
filling belong here too.

Name an authority only when the source names it. Preserve an unsupported source
claim during a prose-only rewrite and flag it separately rather than inventing
support or silently deleting it.

## Formulaic framing

Common structures include:

- throat clearing: "here's the thing", "let's dive in", "it is worth noting";
- staged revelation: "the real question", "at its core", "what matters is";
- binary pivots: "not X but Y", "X is not the problem; Y is";
- negative runways that list what something is not before saying what it is;
- rhetorical questions answered immediately;
- generic challenge, outlook, or upbeat conclusion sections;
- meta-commentary that announces what the document will do.

Repair by starting at the claim. Keep a contrast or question when the logic
actually depends on it or the speaker habitually writes that way.

## Generic language and false precision

Watch for stacked AI-favored abstractions such as *landscape*, *interplay*,
*tapestry*, *pivotal*, *crucial*, *showcase*, and *underscore*. Also watch for
false ranges, synonym cycling, uniformly hyphenated compounds, padded three-item
lists, vague extremes, and conclusions that could end any document.

No word is banned in isolation. Prefer the ordinary term when it carries the
same meaning. Repeat a precise noun instead of cycling through synonyms.

## Synthetic rhythm

Patterns include metronomic medium-length sentences, repeated paragraph shapes,
dramatic fragments, manufactured punchlines, aphorism formulas, and every
paragraph ending on a quotable one-liner. Em dashes, boldface, title case,
inline-header lists, emojis, and curly quotation marks can contribute when they
cluster with the same overproduced rhythm.

Vary rhythm only as the voice and content require. A single short sentence,
dash, or polished paragraph is not a defect.

## Hidden actors and false agency

Passive voice can hide responsibility. Inanimate subjects can also pretend to
act: a decision "emerges", data "speaks", a market "rewards", or a complaint
"becomes" a fix.

Name the actor when doing so improves truth and clarity. Keep passive voice when
the actor is unknown, irrelevant, intentionally protected, or less important
than the action.

## Chatbot residue

Remove collaborative artifacts that leaked into the prose: sycophantic
openings, automatic agreement, "I hope this helps", offers to continue, generic
reassurance, and tutorial narration. Preserve real correspondence conventions
when the artifact is a message or letter.

## Diff-anchored writing

Stable documentation should describe the system as it is. Phrases such as
"this was added", "the previous approach", and "now supports" require a change
context that future readers may not have. Keep that framing in changelogs,
release notes, migrations, and historical analysis.

## Sterile cleanup

Over-editing creates another synthetic voice: uniform grammar, bloodless
neutrality, flattened opinions, removed asides, and suspiciously perfect
organization. Protect:

- specific and unusual detail;
- mixed feelings and unresolved tension;
- deliberate repetition and quirks;
- era-bound references and in-group language;
- meaningful hedging and uncertainty;
- technical, legal, academic, or accessibility conventions.

The goal is credible authorship, not statistical distance from an AI detector.
