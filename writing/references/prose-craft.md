# Prose craft and voice

Read this method for substantive prose whose expression, rhythm or voice needs work. Its default is conservative editing: repair avoidable friction and clustered synthetic patterns while preserving meaning and recognizable authorship. Use a review-only pass when critique is requested. Read [strict style](strict-style.md) only when a strict house contract is selected, such as a request for strict editing, Stop Slop or a hard house-style pass.

## Protect the source before changing expression

Pin the claims, uncertainty, facts, numbers, names, dates, terms, quotations, citation/link targets, argument dependencies, register and required format. Preserve frontmatter, code, data and other non-prose structures unless their edit is in scope. Source meaning comes before cosmetic polish. Current user corrections and explicit editing instructions can change the protected content; a request for voice alone cannot.

Use actual samples as voice authority when available. Notice vocabulary, punctuation, sentence rhythm, paragraph shape, recurring phrases and deliberate quirks. Match the actual speaker and genre rather than making every author sound casually confident. Technical, legal, academic and accessibility conventions carry their own obligations. Current user instructions govern house style, including the no-em-dash rule for generated copy.

Never invent specificity to make writing feel human. Do not silently strengthen, weaken or delete a claim because it sounds awkward. Preserve an unsupported source claim during a prose-only rewrite and flag the substantive issue separately when useful. For newly authored factual claims, obtain support or keep the uncertainty explicit.

## Diagnose a real expression problem

Use the pattern examples below as diagnostic classes. A watched word matters when its use, frequency and neighbors create a pattern. One formal word, passive sentence or polished paragraph does not prove synthetic writing. Change a repeated pattern, a clear voice mismatch or a concrete obstacle for the intended reader.

Keep detail that is hard to fabricate, mixed feelings, unresolved tension, defensible first-person choices, real asides, humor and distinctive rhythm. Protect artistic oddness and meaningful repetition. Cleaning every quirk into uniform grammar can create another artificial voice.

For explanatory, product and technical prose, test generic value claims by swapping the subject. If the sentence could describe an unrelated product unchanged, use a source-backed mechanism, constraint or observation when present. If the source supplies no such detail, do not invent proof to improve the sentence.

## Rewrite at the requested depth

Lead with the point the reader needs, then connect reasons and mechanisms to their consequences. Prefer concrete nouns, direct verbs and simple constructions where they preserve meaning. Merge, split, compress or expand sentences when the emphasis and causal relationships remain faithful. Use sentence length that fits the speaker and content instead of manufacturing alternating rhythm.

Remove ceremony that announces a claim without making it. Repeat the precise noun rather than cycling through synonyms. Name the actor when responsibility matters and the source identifies one. Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, protected or less important than the action. Preserve necessary hedging, adverbs, repetition, terminology and explanation when they carry scope, sequence, uncertainty or voice.

Help the reader avoid unnecessary backtracking. Place a condition near the claim it governs, resolve ambiguous pronouns when the source supplies the referent, and put dependencies before the conclusion that needs them. Preserve a deliberate reveal or dense passage when the genre or authoritative sample calls for it.

Apply punctuation constraints by changing sentence structure. Removing a dash may require a full sentence or a reordered clause; swapping it for parentheses or a colon can retain the same awkward aside. Preserve protected quotations and code. Use headings, bullets, bold and emojis according to the artifact's function and speaker rather than to create decoration or a uniform pattern.

## Compare the draft with the source

Check facts, conditions, certainty, claims, argument dependencies, names, numbers, dates, protected quotes, citations, links and structure. Check that a real opinion, tension, quirk or domain term has not been flattened. Read the draft aloud for this speaker and genre; repair remaining synthetic clusters without forcing visible variety.

If a substantive issue needs author judgment or new facts, keep the source meaning and name the issue. A smoother sentence cannot excuse lost uncertainty or fabricated support. A numeric detector score cannot establish voice quality or faithful meaning. Deliver the requested prose or critique without a mandatory report of the internal drafting steps.

## Pattern examples

Use these as diagnostic classes. A watched word is evidence only when its use,
frequency, and neighbors create a pattern.

### Inflated importance and promotion

Watch for claims that ordinary facts mark a pivotal shift, testify to a legacy,
reflect a broader movement, or reveal a vibrant landscape. Promotional
adjectives and notability lists often perform the same inflation.

Repair by stating the supported fact and its real consequence. Preserve genuine
significance when the source establishes it.

> Before: The launch marks a pivotal moment in the evolving analytics landscape.
>
> After: The launch gives analysts access to the new dataset.

### Vague authority and fabricated depth

Watch for unnamed experts, observers, industry reports, unsupported
attributions, and participial tails such as "ensuring reliability" or
"underscoring its importance." Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers and speculative gap
filling belong here too.

Name an authority only when the source names it. Preserve an unsupported source
claim during a prose-only rewrite and flag it separately rather than inventing
support or silently deleting it.

### Formulaic framing

Common structures include:

- throat clearing: "here's the thing", "let's dive in", "it is worth noting";
- staged revelation: "the real question", "at its core", "what matters is";
- binary pivots: "not X but Y", "X is not the problem; Y is";
- negative runways that list what something is not before saying what it is;
- rhetorical questions answered immediately;
- generic challenge, outlook, or upbeat conclusion sections;
- meta-commentary that announces what the document will do.

Repair by starting at the claim. Keep a contrast or question when the logic
actually depends on it or the speaker habitually writes that way.

### Generic language and false precision

Watch for stacked AI-favored abstractions such as *landscape*, *interplay*,
*tapestry*, *pivotal*, *crucial*, *showcase*, and *underscore*. Also watch for
false ranges, synonym cycling, uniformly hyphenated compounds, padded three-item
lists, vague extremes, and conclusions that could end any document.

No word is banned in isolation. Prefer the ordinary term when it carries the
same meaning. Repeat a precise noun instead of cycling through synonyms.

In explanatory, product, and technical prose, test generic value claims by
swapping the subject. If the sentence could describe an unrelated product
unchanged, prefer the source-backed mechanism, constraint, or measurement. If
the source does not supply one, preserve the claim and flag the missing support
rather than inventing proof.

### Synthetic rhythm

Patterns include metronomic medium-length sentences, repeated paragraph shapes,
dramatic fragments, manufactured punchlines, aphorism formulas, and every
paragraph ending on a quotable one-liner. Em dashes, boldface, title case,
inline-header lists, emojis, and curly quotation marks can contribute when they
cluster with the same overproduced rhythm.

Vary rhythm only as the voice and content require. A single short sentence,
dash, or polished paragraph is not a defect.

### Hidden actors and false agency

Passive voice can hide responsibility. Inanimate subjects can also pretend to
act: a decision "emerges", data "speaks", a market "rewards", or a complaint
"becomes" a fix.

Name the actor when doing so improves truth and clarity. Keep passive voice when
the actor is unknown, irrelevant, intentionally protected, or less important
than the action.

### Chatbot residue

Remove collaborative artifacts that leaked into the prose: sycophantic
openings, automatic agreement, "I hope this helps", offers to continue, generic
reassurance, and tutorial narration. Preserve real correspondence conventions
when the artifact is a message or letter.

### Diff-anchored writing

Stable documentation should describe the system as it is. Phrases such as
"this was added", "the previous approach", and "now supports" require a change
context that future readers may not have. Keep that framing in changelogs,
release notes, migrations, and historical analysis.

### Sterile cleanup

Over-editing creates another synthetic voice: uniform grammar, bloodless
neutrality, flattened opinions, removed asides, and suspiciously perfect
organization. Protect:

- specific and unusual detail;
- mixed feelings and unresolved tension;
- deliberate repetition and quirks;
- era-bound references and in-group language;
- meaningful hedging and uncertainty;
- technical, legal, academic, or accessibility conventions.

The goal is credible authorship, not statistical distance from an AI detector.
