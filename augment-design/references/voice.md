# Voice

Augment's writing stays concrete and calm, and points at its sources. The reader
is a professional who will check.

## Fundamentals

- Sentence case everywhere: headings, buttons, body, labels.
- The reader is *you*. The product is *Augment* or *the platform*. Never *the
  agent*, *the copilot*, or *the AI*. Keep marketing *we* out of product surfaces.
- Actions name the act: "Run", "Apply", "Review changes", "Revert". Use "Get
  started" only where it is literally the start.
- Claims carry evidence: a source, a number, a record, or a consequence.
  "Resolved 12 of 14 items, with sources" beats "Powerful intelligence".
- States are factual. An error gives the fact, the consequence, and the recovery
  path. An empty state names what is missing and the next move.
- No emoji. No exclamation points.
- Keep customer names private unless someone has explicitly approved them.

## Writing for generated artifacts

A generated document, brief, or report follows one extra rule, and it replaces a
long list of banned phrases.

**Copy describes what the reader can inspect, do, or decide. It does not describe
how the artifact was produced.**

The reader did not commission the run, and narrating its provenance spends their
attention on machinery. Naming the pipeline, the model, the safeguards, or the
reason a section exists all do that.

The rule forbids "here is what the system generated", "nothing sends without your
approval", and "confirm or correct this read". It also forbids any future
phrasing of the same move, including plain-language versions that no denylist
would catch. It permits the same information stated as a fact the reader can act
on. An unsent draft is a draft, and saying so once is enough.

## The prose loop

Run this over every string before delivery. It is Augment's local voice floor.
Invoke **humanize** in strict mode for a full prose pass and **simplify** when the
artifact also needs structural editing.

Cut:

- Promotional adjectives and significance claims. Let the fact rank itself.
- Throat-clearing and signposting: "it's worth noting", "importantly", "let's
  look at", "in this section".
- Meta-commentary about the writing or the work.
- Words the context already carries.
- Em dashes and en dashes. Use a period, a comma, a colon, or parentheses.

Rewrite:

- Binary contrasts ("not X, but Y", "X isn't just Y") into the direct claim.
- Negative parallelism and tailing negations ("not because X, but because Y", or
  a clipped "…, no guessing") into what is true.
- Rule-of-three lists padded to three. Two is a fine number.
- False ranges, where "from X to Y" spans nothing.
- Present participles bolted onto a clause: "…, ensuring reliability", "…,
  enabling teams to move faster".
- Aphorism formulas: "the best X is the one that Y".
- Fragmented headers, meaning sentences broken by punctuation for rhythm.
- Passive constructions and subjectless fragments. Name who acts.

Check:

- One word per concept, used the same way throughout.
- Every sentence survives deletion testing. Remove it, and something is lost.
- A conclusion that could end any document is not a conclusion.
- Read it aloud. Where the rhythm runs too even, a machine wrote it.

## Illustrative voice

These teach the voice, not a product. Substitute real nouns.

> **Heading:** Everything worth knowing, in one view
> **Sub:** Augment reads every connected source and surfaces what changed, with
> the records, numbers, and history behind it.
> **Button:** Connect a source
> **Empty:** Nothing here yet. Connect a source and results appear.
> **Error:** Couldn't reach the source. Your last results are still shown. Retry.
